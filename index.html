Oscar Grant · Fruitvale Station · January 1, 2009

One death in Oakland became a movie. Remember the title.

On New Year's Day 2009, BART police officer Johannes Mehserle shot Oscar Grant, a 22-year-old Black man, on the platform at the Fruitvale BART Station in Oakland. Grant was unarmed, face-down, held by another officer. Mehserle said he meant to grab his Taser and grabbed his gun instead. Mehserle was convicted of involuntary manslaughter and served 11 months.

Advance Peace · The Mob Program

A government program where you pay Black men cash not to murder you.

Advance Peace began in Richmond, California. The program identifies the small cohort of men in a city most likely to shoot or be shot, enrolls them in an 18-month "Peacemaker Fellowship," and pays them monthly cash stipends. In Richmond, across the full period since the program launched, homicides have fallen roughly 83% from their 2006 peak (47 in 2006, 8 in 2023).

But Richmond's Office of Neighborhood Safety was created in 2006 and its work predated the Peacemaker Fellowship by several years. Much of the decline occurred before the paid-fellowship component launched in June 2010. The best independent evaluation, published in the American Journal of Public Health, attributes a 55% reduction in firearm violence to the post-2010 program specifically, while the broader citywide decline is confounded by concurrent policing and economic factors. The model was exported to Sacramento anyway.

Proposition 57 · 2016

The law that calls domestic violence nonviolent.

Proposition 57 passed in California in 2016 under the framing of prison overcrowding and racial justice. It authorized earlier parole for offenders convicted of "nonviolent" felonies. Under California's statutory definitions retained by Prop 57, "nonviolent" includes domestic violence, drive-by shootings, assault with a deadly weapon, and rape of an unconscious victim. The list is not a typo. The measure did not redefine these offenses — it accepted the existing narrow statutory category of "violent" (which covers only a handful of specific offenses) and expanded early release to everything outside that list.

For prisoners serving time for an offense in the newly expanded "nonviolent" bucket, good-time credit eligibility rose from 20% to 50%. Sentences effectively halved. DAs were not required to be notified of the resulting early releases.
